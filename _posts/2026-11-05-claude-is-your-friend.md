This summer I gave a lunch and learn at my coworking space called "Claude is Your Friend." This is the written version, mostly for anyone who figures AI isn't for them because they aren't a programmer.

The first step is the easiest one, which is to pay the $20. The free tier is fine for kicking the tires, but I didn't really get what this thing could do until I was using the real version every day.

Once you're paying, you'll notice there's a whole menu of products to choose from, like Code, Cowork, Dispatch, Routines, Design, yada, yada, yada. This is where I'd tell you not to believe the marketing, because the one with the scariest name is actually the most powerful. Claude Code sounds like it's only for programmers, but you talk to it in plain English just like the chat app. The difference is that it can actually do things on your computer instead of just telling you how. I'd bet money it won't be called "Code" forever.

Now, there is one part of the marketing I do believe, and it's that this is here to stay. Remember when HTML was something people talked about? Most of us never learned what it was, and we all ended up on the internet anyway. GPT and LLM are headed the same way, so you don't need to understand the acronyms to get value out of it. You just need to start, and to be a little careful about what you hand over. Don't give it the nuclear codes.

When you do start, the thing that will probably surprise you is how easily it gives up. It'll tell you something can't be done, or hand you a watered down version of what you asked for. That's when I channel my inner Steve Jobs and push back, and more often than not it finds a way. When it truly can't do something, it's usually because nobody has built that piece for it yet, and that piece could be built by YOU.

Which brings me to my favorite slide, titled "Be a tool." Yes, I know how that sounds. The tools are how you fill in those gaps. MCPs connect Claude to your stuff, like your email and calendar. Skills and hooks teach it how you like things done, so you aren't repeating yourself. Agents handle the grunt work, and I run mine in the background so I can keep working while they grind, then check on them from my phone with Remote Control. Add the Claude Chrome extension and there isn't much left that it can't do, which is probably why Mac Minis are so hard to find right now.

If there's one thing I'm sure of, it's that AI isn't going away, so we might as well adapt.

And if you have questions, ask me. My answer will probably be, I don't know... yet.

Thanks for stopping by.
